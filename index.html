Metal: from nugget to single crystal

For ten thousand years, people have been learning to work metal. This book follows their discoveries and the changes inside the metal. Each chapter begins with something people noticed. Then we try their method, explore what they thought was happening, and look inside to see what really happens.

  1. 1 about 8500–7500 BCE
    Anatolia, the Near East

    The stone that would not chip

    Native copper dents under a blow. Then it hardens and cracks. Meet atoms, the tiny balls that make up matter.

  2. 2 about 8200–7500 BCE
    the same region

    Fire makes copper soft again

    Annealing makes metal soft again. See what heat does to folds in the rows of atoms, and what temperature tells us about their movement. Learn how to anneal well.

  3. 3 about 5000 BCE
    the Balkans, Iran

    Metal from stone

    Smelting gets metal out of ore. Casting gives melted metal a shape. Our first chemistry: one substance takes oxygen from another.

  4. 4 3300–1200 BCE

    Bronze

    Why is a mixture of two soft metals harder than either metal alone?

  5. 5 from 1200 BCE

    Iron from the furnace

    The bloomery furnace and the bloom it produces: metal shaped by hammering, without melting it.

  6. 6 antiquity and the Middle Ages

    Steel, hardening and tempering

    Carbon in iron. Bulat and pattern-welded Damascus steel.

  7. 7 Bohemia, 1403

    A forge in the time of Kingdom Come

    The waterwheel, blast furnace, cast iron and craft guilds.

  8. 8 sixteenth–eighteenth centuries

    From alchemy to chemistry

    Assayers, Agricola, phlogiston and Lavoisier. How people found oxygen and carbon.

  9. 9 nineteenth century

    Rivers of steel, and metal under the microscope

    Bessemer, grains and the iron–carbon diagram.

  10. 10 twentieth century and beyond

    Designing the structure

    X-rays, dislocations and stainless steel. Turbine blades made of a single crystal.
